What an hour of you is worth

Profit divided by hours worked. That's the number that tells the truth.

Profit is what you kept, not what came in. Count every hour. Evenings, weekends, checking email from the truck.
What this number carries

Revenue is a vanity number. It says how much came in. Profit divided by hours says whether you're moving toward freedom or deeper into the trade of time for money, and the trade is usually getting worse while revenue grows.

One number, all of it. Your profit, your expenses, your team, your systems, your marketing, your sales, and what an hour of your own time actually returns. If any one of those is sick, this number drops. In Man on a Mission it's one of the three numbers we measure every month.

If the number surprises you, it's supposed to. Almost nobody tracks their hours honestly. The number forces it.

How to read it

Feed it means the rate beats yours. Every hour you free up goes here first. Fix it means it earns between half your rate and your rate. Raise the price or cut the hours before you touch anything else. Cut or delegate means it earns under half your rate. It's paying you to leave.

Years change the verdict. Under two years in, a low rate is Founder math. Hold it, but put a date on it. Six years in and still under half your rate, it isn't a season. It's costing you the years.

Time, money, energy. Everything you hold spends all three. This screen prices the first two. The audit on screen one is where the third one shows up.

Three ways off your plate

Delegate. Someone does it for less than your hour is worth. Automate. A system does it and nobody's hours are spent. Eliminate. It stops, and nothing breaks.

The line. Time cost is hours times the gap between your rate and what you'd pay someone. Above ten percent of that cost, keep it. Five to ten, consider handing it off. Under five, hand it off this month.
